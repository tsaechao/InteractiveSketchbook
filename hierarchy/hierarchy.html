Purple Hibiscus

Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie

2003

Things started to fall apart at home when my brother,

Jaja, did not go to communion and Papa flung his heavy missal across the room and broke the figurines on the étagère.

We had just returned from church.
Mama placed the fresh palm fronds, which were wet with holy water, on the dining table and then went upstairs to change.

Later, she would knot the palm fronds into sagging cross shapes and hang them on the wall beside our gold-framed family photo.


They would stay there until next Ash Wednesday, when we would take the fronds to church, to have them burned for ash.


Papa, wearing a long, gray robe like the rest of the oblates, helped distribute ash every year. His line moved the slowest because he pressed hard on each forehead to make a perfect cross with ash-covered thumb and slowly, meaningfully enunciated every word of "dust and unto dust you shall return."

I'm now starting to get the feel for the basics, at least I hope, on Visual Studio Code. Hierarchy is something that was emphasized a lot in my basic graphic design courses in high school so I'm fairly familiar with what it is. The point of the exercise is to create anarchy where there is usually hierarchy but looking at the page I made, I am a bit peeved. It was a fun exercise though, and although I'm not fully confident, you get the jist on how to adjust things on the page with the activity. This was also a good reminder on how important hierarchy is when delivering info or a message, as it's been a while since I've actually heard and seen the term again. I think for some people who have been doing design or just art in general, hierarchy comes naturally in a way? But seeing disruption in hierarchy really puts into perspective just how important it is, which I guess is something that most people don't think about much on a daily basis. As intended, the text is disrupted and the original message of the excerpt feels less fluid, emphasizing different words and breaking up the text where there originally wasn't a break. It's the same content but it does completely change how it's read and probably interpreted.
